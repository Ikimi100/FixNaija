Guard your polling unit. Protect every vote.

Elections are won — and protected — one polling unit at a time, and Nigeria has 176,846 of them. Sign up to stand for the Adebayo–Bugaje ticket at yours on election day. We'll train you for it.

Election day, step by step

What a polling unit agent does

You are the ticket's eyes and ears at one polling unit — calm, informed and there from the first voter to the last signature.

01Before the day

Get trained

Your coordinator trains you on the voting process, the BVAS machine and what to watch for, and explains how agents are nominated.

02Early morning

Arrive early

Be at your polling unit before voting starts. Watch the set-up, the BVAS machine and the empty ballot box.

03During voting

Watch every step

Observe accreditation and voting. Note anything irregular — calmly, in writing, with the time — and tell your coordinator.

04The count

Guard the result

Stay for the count. Check the result sheet (Form EC8A) matches what you saw, photograph the posted copy and send it in.

Pick your post

Four ways to protect the vote

🗳️

Polling unit agent

Watch voting and counting at one polling unit — ideally the one where you vote.

🧾

Ward collation agent

Follow the results from every polling unit in your ward to the ward collation centre.

🏛️

LGA collation agent

For experienced hands: follow ward results to the LGA collation centre.

🏃

Reserve / runner

Stand by to replace an absent agent, move result photos and support your team on the day.

Questions, answered

Before you sign up

Is this paid?
It is volunteer civic service. Your coordinator will explain the arrangements for your area during training.
Do I have to vote at the same polling unit?
It helps — you can vote and serve in the same place. If you are registered elsewhere, tell us and coordinators will match you to a unit that works.
Who officially appoints agents?
Party agents are formally nominated by the party and their details are submitted to INEC before the election. Signing up here puts your name forward; your coordinator will guide you through training and nomination.
What if something goes wrong on the day?
Stay calm and safe. Write down what happened and the time, photograph what you are allowed to, and report to your coordinator straight away. Never get into an argument or a fight — your job is to watch and record.
Why do you need my photograph?
When the party submits its agents to INEC, each agent's details go with a passport photograph. Use a recent, clear photo of your face on a plain background — the page shapes it to passport size for you.
How is my information used?
Only to organise training, election-day duties and your nomination as a party agent — your details and photograph go to the party and INEC for that, and nowhere else. They are never sold, and your photo is never shown on the website. See our Privacy Policy.
